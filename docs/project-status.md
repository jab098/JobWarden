Task 25b fixes the UK eligibility classifier, which had been discarding most of JobWarden's job stock since Task 2. `assessLocation` treated any location label it did not recognise as evidence _against_ the UK, and its fallback was `non_uk`. Recognition was a hand-written allowlist of 27 cities and 9 official regions, so the county in the "Town, County" form almost every UK advert uses counted as a contradictory qualifier and the advert was positively excluded. Measured over the 230-place dataset Task 25 already ships, with a neutral description: 204 of 230 places (88.7%) were excluded written as `Town` or `Town, Nation`, and 220 of 230 (95.7%) written as `Town, County` or `Town, County, Nation`. `Salford, England` names England outright and was still excluded. All four figures are now zero, and all 230 places publish in all four formats. The defect stayed hidden because the outcome was `excluded` rather than `quarantined`: `supabase/functions/ingest-jobs/handler.ts` skips both identically, nothing is persisted, and the source run record counts only `receivedCount` and `eligibleCount`, so a source discarding 95% of its stock read as a source without much UK content. `classifyUkEligibility` also returns on a `non_uk` location before reading the description, so "based in our Salford office in the UK" could not rescue it.

What changed is the consequence of an unrecognised label, not the test for one. The first implementation made unrecognised labels neutral and moved the entire burden onto a denylist of foreign places; independent review rejected it, and was right to, because publication then depended on the _absence_ of a denylist entry. Three verified ways through it: `isForeignLabel` matched on trim-and-lowercase while the UK side stripped punctuation, so 25 of 28 punctuated forms published including `London, Ont.`, `Manchester, N.H.` and `Bath, U.S.A.`; four subdivisions were missing, so `Bangor, ME`, `Newport, OR` and `Brighton, Victoria` published; and 135 of 136 probed qualifiers were absent entirely, including all New Zealand regions, all Irish counties and some sixty countries, because a denylist of foreign places cannot be enumerated. The remediation restores the original allowlist test and changes only what happens when it fails, to `ambiguous`, which `normalise.ts` already routes to `quarantined`. Publication now requires every label to be positively recognised, so `London, Ont.` is refused because `ont.` is unknown rather than because anyone remembered to list it. Recognition widened to match: exact per-label lookup against the 230-place gazetteer (deliberately not `resolveUkPlaces`, which matches contained names and reads "New York City" as York), 160 administrative areas derived from the dataset's own `county` and `region` fields with multi-name values indexed part-wise, and a hand list of 47 ceremonial counties, because the dataset stores the unitary authority — Leeds's county is "Leeds" — and cannot supply `West Yorkshire`. Of the 27-city allowlist, 25 became redundant and were deleted; `Derry` and `Newcastle` remain because the gazetteer spells them `Londonderry` and `Newcastle upon Tyne`. The denylist survives only as defence in depth, to answer `non_uk` where the answer is known rather than clog the review queue, and because it is no longer load-bearing its entries are omitted rather than risked: `washington` was removed (a town of 67,000 in Tyne and Wear that the first implementation excluded even when the advert said England), `victoria` was never added (it names a district of London), and of the subdivision codes only those that are not also UK postcode areas are included, so `Derby, DE` and `Bangor, ME` quarantine as unrecognised instead of being discarded as foreign.

One limit is documented rather than asserted away: a location whose every label is a real UK place name cannot be told from a UK one by name alone. `Lincoln, Canterbury` and `Oxford, Canterbury`, both real New Zealand towns, publish because Canterbury is also a city in Kent, as does `Newport, Manchester`, a parish in Jamaica. Naming a country closes it — `Lincoln, Canterbury, New Zealand` is `non_uk` — so the exposure is a two-label advert naming no country. Closing it properly would require qualifier positions to be administrative areas rather than any UK place name, which was measured to cost `London, Canary Wharf` and `Manchester, Salford Quays`. A behaviour change worth recording: because a `non_uk` location outcome is now strictly rarer, a location such as `Amsterdam` paired with a description saying "Applicants must be based in the UK" now publishes where it was previously excluded on the location alone; that follows the specification, since explicit UK eligibility evidence in the advert body is what the description rules exist to read, but it was not an intended part of this change.
